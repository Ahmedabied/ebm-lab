# 04 — The body is the only teacher
voice: kokoro
length-target: ~6 min

## p01 | viz: loop diagram, Body box lit. The bowl with axes: E from 0 to 1, X from 0 to 0.3. The ball at (0.8, 0). The formula D = (1 − E)² + X² appears term by term as spoken.
Third box: the body. It carries two numbers, energy E and toxin X. From them it computes one number, the drive D, how far it is from comfortable. Comfortable is energy one, toxin zero. The formula: one minus E, squared, plus X squared.

## p02 | viz: WHY SQUARE. Two curves against E: the plain distance |1−E| and the square (1−E)². A slider on E moves a dot along both at once with the two heights read out live; as E slides from 0.9 to 0.6 the plain one goes 0.1 → 0.4, the square 0.01 → 0.16.
Why squared and not just the distance? Watch the two curves as energy slides from zero point nine down to zero point six. The plain distance goes from zero point one to zero point four, four times. The square goes from zero point zero one to zero point one six, sixteen times. Hunger gets urgent fast. A car's stopping distance works like this: twice the speed, four times the distance. Hunger here works the same way.

## p03 | viz: the same two curves near E = 1: the square is flat at the bottom; the plain distance has a kink. A note: "the code takes the absolute distance first, then squares".
Two more reasons. The code takes the absolute distance first, so over or under the setpoint reads the same, and the square then makes the far side steep. And at the bottom the square is flat, so a tiny wobble around comfortable costs almost nothing. That flat bottom and steep walls is what a bowl looks like.

## p04 | viz: WHY A SUM. The two terms as two walls of the bowl; the ball slides up the hunger wall, or the poison wall, or both.
Why add the two terms? Because two discomforts should add up. Hungry and poisoned is worse than either alone. Each term is its own wall of the bowl, and the sum is the bowl.

## p05 | viz: PAUSE AND PREDICT card: "One bite of 0.05 at energy 0.78, or the same bite at energy 0.95. Which teaches more?" Then both balls on the bowl, both drops marked: from 0.78: 0.0484 → 0.0289, a drop of 0.0195; from 0.95: 0.0025 → 0.0000, a drop of 0.0025.
Pause and predict. The creature eats one bite of zero point zero five at energy zero point seven eight. Then imagine it eats the same bite at zero point nine five. Which bite teaches more? The first, by far: a drop of about two hundredths against a drop of about two thousandths. The bowl is steep up the wall and flat at the bottom. That is the square at work: the same bite is a bigger lesson when you are hungrier.

## p06 | viz: THE LESSON, real. Tick 14 of seed 203 shown at full precision: D before 0.04915, the bite lands, E +0.05, D after 0.03002, r = 0.04915 − 0.03002 = 0.01913 (the dump's unrounded r 0.01914). The ball slides down the bowl and the drop is marked.
The lesson r is the drive before the tick minus the drive after it. A drop is a positive lesson. Here is the first bite of seed two hundred and three's life, at tick fourteen. Energy goes up by zero point zero five, the drive falls from zero point zero four nine to zero point zero three zero, and the lesson is plus zero point zero one nine. Nobody says "that was food". The brain sees a positive number.

## p07 | viz: METABOLISM. The cost per moving tick built up: 0.0005 resting + 0.001 moving + 4 × 0.0000125 brain = 0.00155. E ticks down by that; D ticks up; r = −0.0006. A strip of 50 quiet ticks, every one a small red bar.
Every tick, even a quiet one, costs. Resting costs zero point zero zero zero five. Moving costs another zero point zero zero one. Each unit in the brain costs a sliver more, so a bigger brain is a hungrier brain. Together, about one and a half thousandths of energy per tick. That pushes the ball a little up the hunger wall every tick, so the lesson on a quiet tick is slightly negative, about minus six ten-thousandths. Ninety-one percent of this creature's waking ticks had a negative lesson. Doing nothing is never free. That is what makes food worth finding.

## p08 | viz: POISON. Tick 192: X jumps from 0 to 0.025 (0.5 × 0.05); D rises by X² plus the usual metabolic creep; r = −0.00079. Then over the next ticks X decays by 2% per tick while E bleeds 0.01 × X per tick. The ball jumps up the poison wall, then slides down it while creeping up the hunger wall.
A poison bite does two things. Toxin jumps by half the mass eaten, so one bite adds zero point zero two five. That is the ball shoved up the poison wall, and a negative lesson, minus eight ten-thousandths on this tick. Then every tick after, the toxin fades by two percent, and while it is there it drains energy, one percent of the toxin per tick. So poison is a small immediate pain and a slow bleed. In this world, at this toxicity, the bleed is survivable. Keep that for chapter seven, where no brain learns to avoid poison.

## p09 | viz: DEATH. E runs down to zero; the creature greys out. E is capped at 1.0: a bite at full energy is wasted (the real tick 1231: E 1.0 before and after, r = 0).
Death is one rule: energy at or below zero. There is no separate poison death; poison kills by draining energy. And energy is capped at one, so eating when full is wasted. Here is seed two hundred and three doing exactly that at tick one two three one: a bite with a full tank, and a lesson of zero. Those two lines are the entire life-and-death physics.

## p10 | viz: loop diagram: Body box filled; the arrow out is labelled "r, one number, every tick" and goes to Learning; a second arrow from Body goes back into Senses, labelled "E, X".
Connections. The body never reads the smell. It only receives what was eaten. It gives two things back: its energy and toxin, which go into the senses as two of the twelve readings, and the lesson r, which goes to the learning rule and nowhere else. Stripped down: the body is a bowl, and the lesson is how far the ball rolled toward the bottom this tick.

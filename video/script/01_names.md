# 01 — The names
voice: eleven
length-target: ~9 min

## p01 | viz: title card "The names". Thirteen words appear as empty slots on the loop diagram.
Thirteen words. Each one is a thing you can picture. For each I give you the picture first, then where it lives in the creature. The maths comes in the next chapter, once the words are solid.

## p02 | viz: ENERGY. Three price tags, one per move, the real Q of tick 3693 printed under them with the sign flipped (F = −Q): LEFT −0.0011, RIGHT +0.0023, FORWARD +0.0016; the cheapest is LEFT. The cheapest tag lights up. Then the legend that stays on every bar screen from now on: "F energy, low wins  |  Q = minus F, high wins".
Energy. In this lab, energy is a price. Three shops sell the same thing, you buy from the cheapest, and when a price changes you may switch shops. The creature's brain gives each of its three moves a price, and takes the cheapest. Low wins. Now the one convention to settle for the whole lesson. We will also talk about evidence, which is the same number with the sign flipped: high wins. Two words, one number, opposite signs. The code's name for the flipped one is Q, and from now on every bar on screen says which of the two it shows.

## p03 | viz: the marble on a bumpy table with three dips, rolling to the lowest. A small caption: "a picture for later, when the table shakes".
One more picture, for later. A marble on a bumpy table rolls to the lowest dip. The dips are the three energies. One warning so the picture stays honest: a real marble takes time to roll. The brain compares three numbers in one instant. The marble is only useful once the table starts shaking, which is the temperature, two words from now.

## p04 | viz: MODEL. A box with knobs on top. Twelve readings enter on the left, three energies exit on the right. A knob turns and the three outputs change live. Counter: "103 knobs at birth".
Model. A model is a machine with knobs. Readings go in, numbers come out, and the knobs decide how. A kitchen radio is a model: the signal in the air goes in, sound comes out, and two knobs, tuning and volume, decide what you hear. One difference: you turn the radio's knobs. Here the body's number turns them, and that is chapter six. Our creature's brain has one hundred and three knobs at birth. Learning means turning them. The one other thing that can change is that on an eventful night the brain grows a new unit, with sixteen knobs of its own, born silent.

## p05 | viz: ENERGY-BASED MODEL. The knob box feeds the three price tags. One output drops and the cheapest tag switches.
Energy-based model. Put the two pictures together. It is a machine with knobs that gives every option a price, and prefers the low ones. Notice what it is not. It is not a machine that announces which option is right. It prices all of them, and the choice is made by comparing. That difference matters when we get to learning, because you can lower one price, or raise the others, and both change the choice.

## p06 | viz: TEMPERATURE. The table under the marble shakes. A dial T from cold to hot. Cold: the marble sits in the deepest dip. Hot: it hops between all three. Beside it, three bars of probability melt toward equal as T rises. PAUSE AND PREDICT card: "The table shakes as hard as it can. Where does the marble spend its time?"
Temperature. If the table shakes, the marble does not always sit in the lowest dip. Temperature is how hard it shakes. Pause and predict: the table shakes as hard as it can. Where does the marble spend its time? Everywhere equally. Three moves, one chance each. Cold table, same move every time. A tired person picks the same chair every evening; a restless one tries every chair. Same chairs, different temperature. In the code the letter is T. One warning so the picture stays honest: the brain that won in your lab does not use the shaking table to act. It uses the dice from the word policy, coming soon. You still need T, because the first rule used it, and because it explains why the first rule lurched.

## p07 | viz: WEIGHT. One knob. A reading (0.521) on the left, the knob value in the middle, the product on the right. Turn the knob positive, negative, zero: the product follows, and an energy bar drops or rises with it.
Weight. A weight is one knob. It says how much one reading counts, and in which direction. Positive: this reading adds evidence for the move, so the move's energy goes down. Negative: it takes evidence away, energy up. Zero: ignore this reading. The brain's main knobs are called w, and there is one for every pair of reading and move: twelve readings, three moves, thirty-six knobs in that block alone.

## p08 | viz: VECTOR. A till receipt, then a column of twelve boxes with their names (contrast A, B, C; mean A, B, C; change A, B, C; energy; toxin; one). Real values from tick 3693 fill in.
Vector. A vector is a list of numbers kept in a fixed order. A till receipt is a vector: a fixed order of items, and the fifth price always belongs to the fifth item. Our creature feels twelve numbers every tick: three left-minus-right contrasts, one per smell; three average levels; three changes since the last tick; its own energy; its own toxin; and a constant one. That list is called x. The order never changes, so knob number four always meets reading number four. That is the only reason the order matters.

## p09 | viz: DOT PRODUCT. A grocery bill: quantities, prices, line totals, the bill. Then the same shape with readings and knobs: each pair multiplies into a third column; the third column collapses into one number.
Dot product. Two lists of the same length. Multiply each pair, then add everything up. One number comes out. A grocery bill does exactly this: quantity of each item times its price, added up. Here the readings are the quantities and the knobs are the prices, and a price can be negative, a refund. Readings times knobs, added up, is a weighted total, and that total is one move's evidence. We will do it on the real numbers in chapter two.

## p10 | viz: GRADIENT. A foggy hill in one dimension (one knob): a dot on the slope, an arrow pointing uphill. Then a 2-D hill (two knobs) with the arrow as a vector. The dot steps against the arrow and sinks. A guitar tuning peg beside it.
Gradient. Stand on a hill in fog. The gradient is the arrow that says which way is steepest up, and how steep. Walk against it and you go down. For the brain, the hill is a mistake score, and the ground is the knobs. The gradient says, for every knob at once: turn it this way to make the mistake smaller. Tuning a guitar is one knob: the note is flat, so you tighten, and how far off you are tells you how much. The brain does that for a hundred strings at once, every tick.

## p11 | viz: LEARNING RATE. The same hill. Small steps: the dot walks down and settles. PAUSE AND PREDICT card: "The valley is one step wide. I take a step ten wide. Where do I land?" Then the huge step: the dot jumps clean over the valley onto the opposite slope, then back, lurching.
Learning rate. How big a step you take against that arrow. Pause and predict: the valley is one step wide and I take a step ten wide. Where do I land? On the far slope, every time, in a new place. Too small a step and you never arrive. Too big and you jump over the valley and land on the other hill. In your first experiment the nightly step was eight to sixteen times bigger than the whole set of knobs, and the brain lurched from one habit to the opposite one every night. How you measure the size of a set of knobs is in chapter two. We will watch the lurch with the real numbers.

## p12 | viz: LESSON. The body's two bars E and X, and one number D above them. A bite: E rises, D drops, a green "+0.01914" pops out. A quiet tick: D creeps up, a tiny red "−0.0006".
Lesson. Other books say reward. Our creature is never told what food is. Its body keeps one number for how uncomfortable it is. The lesson is how much that number dropped this tick. Dropped a lot: good lesson. Rose: bad lesson. In the code the letter is r. That is the only teacher the brain ever gets, and nobody ever labels the bite.

## p13 | viz: DRIVE. A bowl seen from the side, then from above as a contour map with axes E and X. A ball at (0.8, 0). Height of the ball = D. Metabolism nudges the ball up the hunger wall tick by tick; a bite pushes it down; a poison bite shoves it up the other wall.
Drive. That discomfort number has a name: drive. Picture a bowl. The bottom of the bowl is full energy and zero toxin. The ball is where the body is right now. Drive is how high up the bowl the ball sits. Hunger is the ball up one wall; poison is the ball up the other wall. Unlike a real ball, it never rolls down on its own. Metabolism pushes it up every tick. Only eating brings it down. Our creature is born a little way up the hunger wall, at energy zero point eight.

## p14 | viz: VALUE. A row of future ticks drawn as dots on a path; the now tick (a small negative), a food bite twenty ticks ahead. The now bar and the future bars, each farther one shrunk by a factor, stack into one bar labelled Q.
Value. How good is this spot, counting what comes after. Value is what you get now plus what follows, with the far part counting less. A train ticket: you pay now, that is a small negative, and the trip is the positive that follows. Its value counts both. The smell of food is worth almost nothing this tick, but it leads to a bite twenty ticks from now, and value counts that bite, shrunk a little for every tick of distance. The learner that worked in your lab learns values, and its value is Q, the evidence from the first word. Hold that thought.

## p15 | viz: POLICY. The three bars; the highest Q is picked nine times out of ten; one time in ten a dice chooses any of the three. A small note: "the dice lands on the favourite a third of the time, so about one tick in fifteen actually differs".
Policy. The habit of choosing. Given what it feels, which move does it take. The creature's final policy is simple: take the highest Q, and one time in ten roll a dice over all three moves instead, so about one tick in fifteen it actually tries another move. That one in ten is called epsilon. It is the reason you sometimes order a dish you have never tried: you cannot know the menu by only ordering your favourite. Epsilon and temperature are two answers to the same question, how often to try the other moves. Temperature ties it to the gaps between the energies. Epsilon fixes it at one in ten whatever the gaps.

## p16 | viz: all thirteen words snap onto the loop diagram: World → Senses (vector x) → Brain (weights, dot product, energy, Q) → Move (policy: temperature or epsilon) → Body (drive) → Lesson r → Learning (gradient, learning rate, value) → back to Brain.
Now put the words on the loop. The world makes smells. The senses pack them into the vector x. The brain's weights turn x into three evidences, three energies. The policy, inside the move box, picks the highest Q. The body's drive moves, the drop is the lesson r, and the lesson turns the knobs by a gradient step, scaled by the learning rate. Every chapter from here is one box of this picture, and at the end of each chapter we come back to it.

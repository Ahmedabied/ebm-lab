## p01 | viz: TABULAR Q. A table: 54 rows (3 signs per note = 27 patterns × 2 hunger states) × 3 moves, beside the 3 × 12 heat map. A lesson updates one cell in the table and a whole row in the heat map. Beside the table, the sign feature: the contrast 0.52 collapsing to a plus sign.
Before the bars, the yardstick. Tabular Q keeps no knobs and no formula. It keeps a table. It first rounds each contrast to a sign, minus, zero or plus, which side is stronger with the amount thrown away. That is called a sign feature. Three signs make twenty-seven patterns, times hungry or not, fifty-four rows, three numbers in each row, one per move. It learns one cell at a time and never shares what it learned between rows. The energy brain shares twelve knobs per move across every sensation it will ever feel. That is the whole difference.

## p02 | viz: EXP-01 bars (results/01_main_linear/results.json): random 1303, ebm-nolearn 1224, ebm 973, tabular_q 3212, oracle 3616, braitenberg 4072, with 95% CIs. Title: "EXP-01, 20 seeds, 15 days, linear world".
Now the loop runs for real. Your first experiment: twenty seeds, fifteen days each, the rule-one brain. The bars are ticks alive. Random walking lives about thirteen hundred ticks. The brain with learning switched off lives twelve hundred. The brain with learning switched on lives nine hundred and seventy-three. Learning made it worse. Tabular Q, the table learner as it was then written, lived three thousand two hundred. And a hand-wired creature that is simply told "A is good" lived four thousand. Everything the learner needed was there; it just did not find it.

## p03 | viz: the m3 bias flips replay leads (seed 313 +0.99 → −1.00). Then the correction trail from findings/01 as three stacked sentences, each struck through and the next written beneath: "too weak to act on" → "a step 8–16× the weights; cannot unlearn" → "lurches between near-deterministic habits every night".
The record of why is itself a lesson. Here is seed three one three flipping overnight again. The first write-up said the brain learned the right thing too weakly to act on. A second look said the step was eight to sixteen times too large and the policy could not unlearn. A hostile referee then showed that committed policies flip overnight, so "cannot unlearn" was withdrawn too, and the final sentence is: it lurches between near-deterministic habits every night. All three sentences are still in the file, in order. You never delete a wrong claim; you correct it underneath, so the next reader sees how the understanding moved.

## p04 | viz: THE REFEREES. Two silhouettes labelled "break this claim". Two catches as cards with their numbers: A16 "Q starts at 2.8, target 2.5; greedy move loses its top spot 64–69%; pilot 627 → 3729" and A17 "tabular Q built s′ from the pre-move reading: not textbook; tabular_q_next added".
Before the second experiment, every conclusion got two hostile referees: a second agent briefed to break the claim. Two of their catches changed the result. One: the centring from chapter five, without which the Q learner rotated through moves instead of choosing. Two: the table learner was not textbook. It built its "next state" from the reading before the move, so it mostly bootstrapped from itself. It stayed as the pre-registered target, and a corrected version was added as an extra arm. Both were found before the main run. The step-size pilot was re-run on the fixed code; the main run never had to be.

## p05 | viz: EXP-02 design as a row of characters. B0 ebm-dn (online + night). +sign (the sign features). +greedy (highest-Q acting). +critic (a value helper). td (greedy + the promise + online: the value rule). all (td + sign). Beside each, the advantage it borrows from tabular Q.
The second experiment asked one question. Tabular Q beat the energy brain by three to one; it has four advantages. The sign features. Acting on the highest value. The promise, the bootstrapping from chapter six. And learning every tick. Which of these explains the gap? So the energy brain was given each advantage alone, then the value rule, then everything. Every arm was named, and five predictions were written down and committed before a single run.

## p06 | viz: EXP-02 bars (results/02_main_linear/results.json, linear): random 1175, ebm-nolearn 1147, ebm-dn 1145, +greedy 1127, +critic 1457, +sign 1963, ebm-all 2076, tabular_q 2882, tabular_q_next 3919, ebm-td 4113, with CIs; the 5250 ceiling as a dashed line; "15/20 at the ceiling" on ebm-td.
The bars. Greedy acting alone: nothing. A value helper alone: a little. Sign features alone: a real gain, to nineteen hundred. The value rule, Q equals minus F, greedy, learning every tick plus replay: four thousand one hundred, fifteen of twenty lives still alive on day fifteen, the ceiling of the experiment. And everything together, two thousand: worse than the value rule alone, for a reason the referees found and we will get to.

## p07 | viz: the 95% range on each bar explained once: "20 seeds; the range is where the true mean most likely sits". Then the paired difference ebm-td − tabular_q = +1230 [−187, 2552] drawn as an interval crossing zero; vs tabular_q_next +194 [−1200, 1698]. The five predictions scored: P1 failed, P2 failed, P3 held, P4 not supported, P5 held.
Read it the way the referees allow. Twenty seeds is not many, so each bar carries a range: where the true mean most likely sits. If the range of a difference crosses zero, we are not allowed to say which is better. No single borrowed ingredient closes the gap. The value-learning paradigm does. The energy brain reaches tabular Q's level: the paired difference is plus twelve hundred, but its range runs from minus two hundred to plus twenty-five hundred, so it crosses zero. Superiority is not shown. Against the textbook version it is a tie. Of the five predictions, two held, two failed, one was not supported. That is what a real result looks like: the answer, with the parts that are not proven marked as such.

## p08 | viz: THE WEIGHTS NIGHT BY NIGHT. The 3×12 heat map of w from run.json brain[0..14] for seed 203, animated night to night. Then three lines, the LEFT−RIGHT difference per contrast: B positive all life (+0.026 → +0.002); A from −0.030, first positive at tick 708 (third day), wandering around zero on days 6–9, ending small positive; C small negative.
Here is the learning itself, in the brain of seed two hundred and three, one picture per night. Watch the left-minus-right difference for each contrast knob, the only knobs that can steer. The B pair is positive from the first night and stays positive: turn toward the side with more B. The A pair starts negative, turn away from A, crosses zero on the third day, wanders around zero for a few days, and ends small and positive. The C pair stays small and slightly negative. In this seed the steering was carried by B. B leaks from food and poison both. But the world keeps twenty-four units of food mass against eight of poison, so "toward B" pays, and the body rewarded it. Your own world file still says B carries no information. The referee showed that is wrong for the gradient, and research seven records the correction.

## p09 | viz: PAUSE AND PREDICT card: "Freeze the day-5 brain. Swap its antennae, left for right. What happens?" Then the three bars (re-derived: video/data/frozen_brain_test.json, seeds 500–514): frozen day-5 brain 5250, 15/15; mirrored 1013, 0/15; birth brain 1419, 2/15.
Is that learning, or luck? Three tests, re-run for this lesson on fifteen fresh seeds, with the command in the repo. Take this creature's brain as it was after its fifth night, freeze it, no learning, no sleep, no growth, and let it live fifteen new lives. It survives all fifteen, to the ceiling. Pause and predict. We take the same frozen brain and swap its antennae, left for right. What happens? It dies every time, around a thousand ticks. A brain that steers by contrast steers backwards when you mirror it. And the brain it was born with, under the same acting rule: two lucky lives out of fifteen, the rest starve. That the mirrored brain dies every time is the proof that the contrast knobs carry the skill, and that the skill was learned in five days.

## p10 | viz: the day-10 trajectory of seed 203 on the real fields (loops around yellow patches). Action shares as a stacked bar: day 0 L/R/F 0.19/0.29/0.52; day 10 0.48/0.36/0.16. The turn-toward-A ratio per day as a line: 0.81, 0.51, 0.79, 0.73 … 0.82, with day 1 marked "coin toss".
What does the learned creature do? Here is day ten of its life on the real smell map. It circles inside the yellow patches. On day zero half its moves were forward; by day ten it turns on eighty-four percent of ticks. This seed turned toward the higher-A side on four turns in five already on day zero, fell to a coin toss on day one, and was back above three quarters from day two. In the lives the referee traced for the findings, that ratio rose from about a half on day one to about three quarters by day ten. The ratio is noisy, which is why the frozen test, not this ratio, is the proof.

## p11 | viz: LIMITS, first screen: two cards. "No poison avoidance: ebm-td poison rate vs random −0.011, p 0.84." "The committee did not carry the win: frozen brains with v = 0 still reach 5250 on 15/15."
What the referees would not let us say. One: nothing learned to avoid poison. Not the energy brain, not tabular Q. Survivors live by eating more food, and at this toxicity that is enough. Two: the committee did not carry the win. Frozen brains with the committee's output knobs zeroed still reach the ceiling. In this world, the twelve linear knobs did it.

## p12 | viz: LIMITS, second screen: three cards. "ebm-all was a step-size casualty: at 0.1 on fresh seeds 4806." "Ceiling: 15/20 and 9/20 at 5250." "ebm-td either reaches the ceiling or starves: its 5 failures ate ≤ 1 food."
Three: the all-advantages arm was a step-size casualty. With fifteen inputs instead of twelve, the same step blew up on night zero; at a smaller step on fresh seeds it lives forty-eight hundred. So whether sign features help the value rule is not known. Four: fifteen of twenty lives hit the ceiling, which squeezes strong arms together, so the means understate the differences. Five: the value brain either reaches the ceiling or starves early; its five failures ate almost nothing.

## p13 | viz: THE PIVOT (decisions/0002). Five words with a verdict each: learn ✓ (frozen test), function ✓ (three moves from smells), connect (present, unused: needs the xor world, "A and B together is food; A alone or B alone is poison"), born (placeholder: v = 0 units on eventful days), die (missing).
And then you changed the goal. The experiment goal is now the smallest creature with brain cells like a human's. The energy model's goal is to mimic how a cell learns, connects, functions, dies and is born. Scored against that, this experiment showed learn, yes, the frozen test. Function, yes, three moves from smells. Connect: the knobs exist, the world never needed them; that needs the xor world, where food is A and B together and either one alone is poison, which no straight line of knobs can separate, so the committee must do it. Born: a placeholder. Die: not built. The next session starts from that list.

## p14 | viz: the complete loop diagram, every box filled, and the one line.
Connections. Every box on the loop was running: smells to twelve numbers, numbers to three Q, the highest Q to a move, the move to the body's bowl, the drop to the promise, the promise to the knobs. Stripped down: the loop ran. The body's number, through the promise, found the sign of three contrast knobs in five days, and that is all the skill is.

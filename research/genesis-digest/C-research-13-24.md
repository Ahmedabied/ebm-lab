## 1. Per-report digests

### 13 — How a body becomes perceivable (appearance channel)
**Abstract.** Mate choice read candidates' private `energy`/`bond` directly. That broke the project's "mediation law": nothing may act on anything except through a medium. The report designs `world.appear`, a 2-channel field that is rebuilt every tick and never decays (light does not linger). A `_perceive()` read adds distance attenuation and Weber-proportional noise (13:5, 13:17-49). It keeps sight separate from voice so they can be ablated independently (13:57).
**Key findings.** Guilford & Dawkins 1991 split signal *content* from *efficacy* (13:9). Endler 1992/1993: the medium's physics bounds what a signal can carry (13:9). Wiley 2006: receiver performance is a d′ problem (13:9). **Weber's law is the main import**: receivers judge ratios, not differences (Akre & Johnsen 2014; Akre et al. 2011) (13:9). Contested: a 2020 treefrog "opposite miss" paper whose authors were not verified (13:11). The "index signal" status here is enforced by code, not by anatomy (13:11, 13:111).
**Decision.** Option (b): a per-tick rendered field. The scent-decayed field was rejected as "wrong physics" (13:15), and a direct noisy read was rejected as still telepathic (13:53). Measured cost 56-60 µs/tick (13:51). No new genes; an acuity gene was parked until it could be shown to clear drift (13:65). Named gap: repeated noisy sampling over a long courtship averages the noise away (CLT). The suggested fix is a "first impression sticks" cache (13:103).

### 14 — Ontogeny: the body over one life
**Abstract.** Newborns and adults were structurally identical. The design adds `size` (von Bertalanffy growth gated by a slow nutrition EMA), `injury`/`scar`, `parity`, and a zero-state `frailty()` readout (14:5-16, 14:89-117, 14:164-168).
**Key findings.** Von Bertalanffy 1938, Gompertz 1825, Stearns 1992, Roff 1992/2002: maturation is a reaction-norm optimum (14:20-26). Kirkwood 1977 disposable soma (14:28-34). Williams 1957, Hamilton 1966 and Medawar 1952 on antagonistic pleiotropy vs mutation accumulation; Charlesworth & Hughes 1996 give the test that tells them apart (14:36-46). West-Eberhard 2003 and Baldwin 1896; Fontanari & Santos 2017 show that genetic assimilation needs many replicates (14:57-66). Metcalfe & Monaghan 2001 on catch-up growth costs (14:62). Correction made: Mousseau & Roff 1987 mean morphological h²=0.461, not the "0.4-0.8" in the brief (14:68-71).
**Decision.** Two genes (growth rate, heal rate). `MATURE_AGE` was deliberately kept fixed: a maturation-time gene "would give one locus leverage over every other rate in the genome" (14:223-228). Self-caught trap: taxing growth with nothing rewarding size would push the gene to its floor, so a thermal cost of being small was added to make the trade-off two-sided (14:290-297). `frailty` would saturate unless `load` got a recovery term, which was made a hard prerequisite (14:299-305).

### 15 — Preference over bodies (learned preference)
**Abstract.** Adds a learned preference template, blended with the innate weights by one gene `G_IMPRINT_W`. The template is filled by sexual imprinting on the opposite-sex parent and by mate-choice copying weighted by prestige (15:5, 15:65-106). Proposes a cross-fostering ablation to separate genetic from learned preference (15:124-130).
**Key findings.** Ritchie 1996: preference *shape* (directional vs stabilizing) is itself genetic (15:11). Basolo 1990 and Rodd et al. 2002: the preference predates the trait (15:13). Imprinting: Immelmann 1972; ten Cate & Vos 1999; Verzijden et al. 2012 (15:15). Copying: Dugatkin 1992; Witte et al. 2015 (15:17). Contested/flagged findings: averageness, symmetry (Van Dongen & Gangestad 2011, r≈0.18-0.2 with publication bias), WHR universality (contradicted by Yu & Shepard 1998 and Marlowe & Wetsman 2001), masculinity (Scott et al. 2014), the rare-male effect (non-replication by Partridge & Gardner 1983), and DeBruine 2005 (resemblance raises trust, not attraction) (15:25-32).
**Decision.** A convex blend `(1-w)·innate − w·dist²(template)`, which adds the first *stabilizing* (peaked) preference shape (15:69-79). The cue index list is gathered rather than sliced, so many reports can append genes without renumbering everyone else's (15:38-47). The report adopted another report's marker gene instead of shipping "a fourth near-duplicate" (15:57-61, 15:145).

### 16 — Body and self (`rhp`, a self-model)
**Abstract.** One scalar `rhp` (self-assessed standing). It is a leaky EMA toward a target built from the agent's own interoceptive condition plus outcomes it received (bids reciprocated, contests won). It gates choosiness, courting effort and contest strength (16:5-16, 16:117-172).
**Key findings.** Parker 1974 pure self-assessment vs Enquist & Leimar 1983 mutual assessment. Arnott & Elwood 2009: cumulative assessment is the most common pattern (16:22-36). Hsu, Earley & Wolf 2006 winner/loser effects (16:31-34). Leary et al. 1995 and Leary 2005 sociometer: self-esteem as a monitor of *others' responses* (16:45-54). Craig 2002 interoception (16:63-68). Contested: Damasio's somatic marker hypothesis (critique by Dunn et al. 2006), and Sapolsky 2005, where the rank↔stress direction flips under instability (16:72-82).
**Decision.** Five genes. `rhp` itself is learned and resets at birth: "capacity is genetic, content is not" (16:108-113). Named traps: "`rhp` as `energy` in a trenchcoat", meaning the outcome sensitivity collapses to zero and `rhp` becomes smoothed energy (16:281-284). A cross-variable runaway with gloom (16:286-293). Forward note: M2 should replace `rhp` with an EBM's prediction *error* and per-channel precision on social channels (16:367-382).

### 17 — Body and society (arbitrary tags)
**Abstract.** A zero-cost heritable tag `G_TAG` plus three sensitivity genes. The tag is inert in mate choice, giving and social targeting, and load-bearing only in contest escalation (badge of status) (17:11-21, 17:92-147).
**Key findings.** Riolo, Cohen & Axelrod 2001 (year corrected from "1999"), rebutted by Roberts & Sherratt 2002. Antal et al. 2009. Jansen & van Baalen 2006: with free recombination, dominance *cycles* rather than settling ("beard chromodynamics") (17:25-41). Rohwer 1975 and Rohwer & Rohwer 1978 badges. **Correction**: Sánchez-Tójar et al. 2018 meta-analysis finds the house-sparrow bib effect "small and uncertain", with publication bias (17:43-56). Kalick & Hamilton 1986: assortment emerges from market matching with no similarity preference (17:74-82). Schelling 1971 needs a *blank* board, which Genesis is not (17:84-88).
**Decision.** Pre-register falsifiers 1, 4 and 7 as more likely to fail than pass (17:243-246). Use three controls to separate social sorting from patch-following and kinship: a placebo locus, redraw-at-birth, and a patch-conditioned comparison (17:193-207).

### 18 — Attraction as a computation (cue integration)
**Abstract.** Audits `_best_mate`. It is a fully compensatory dot product over cues that are *not* redundant, with flat weights, no search cost, a threshold that cannot learn, and simultaneous mutual consent that is not stable matching (18:32-39). Designs an ornament-vs-eloquence experiment with an index-honesty toggle κ (18:60-68, 18:86-90).
**Key findings.** Candolin 2003: three rival multi-cue hypotheses, evidence "genuinely split" (18:11). Johnstone 1996 (backup signals). Janetos 1980 vs Real 1990: search cost reverses best-of-n vs threshold. Weitzman 1979 "Pandora's Box". Fawcett & Johnstone 2003 (18:12). Ernst & Banks 2002: reliability-weighted integration is the normative benchmark (18:13). Bergstrom & Real 2000: two-sided matching needs different math from one-sided Lande-Kirkpatrick (18:15, 18:150). Young & Wang 2004: attraction is an approach-gating chemical state (18:16).
**Decision.** Keep the threshold rule for M1. The libido-gate architecture is deferred to the M2 EBM (18:151). Traps: dilution mistaken for displacement (18:141). Mate-target dithering under noise, with fix candidates of an incumbent bonus or a per-target accumulator (18:101). Eloquence still telepathic, which can reverse the experiment's sign (18:82, 18:140).

**Abstract.** Should bodies gain a size gene? The report says the risk is exploits and archive math, not compute (19:5).
**Key findings.** Sims 1994: creatures exploited an Euler-integration bug and a collision bug; "bugs will be found and exploited" (19:11). Polyworld (Yaeger 1994) complexity plateaued (19:12). Framsticks, Avida/Tierra (19:13-14). Pfeifer & Bongard 2006 morphological computation: "the body does the work" and "the body gets energy for free" are "the same sentence in a numeric simulator" (19:20). CPPN-NEAT (Stanley 2007) and HyperNEAT 2009 versus flat encodings (19:22). Cully et al. 2015: an archive works as a damage-recovery prior (19:24). CVT-MAP-Elites (Vassiliades et al.) (19:24).
**Decision.** A flat vector can add scalar dials but "cannot grow regular, repeating, or combinatorial structure". Scope stays at 1-3 dials, with generative encoding deferred to M2+ (19:26-32). Proved that any unmatched size→heat coupling widens the free-energy exploit (19:45). Measured that the existing exploit is only break-even under day/night cycling (19:47). Found that the rest refund has no upper energy clip (19:53). Adding a 5th archive axis takes the bins from 625 to 3,125 and crashes coverage as a "pure bin-count artifact" (19:71-85).

### 20 — Salt findability
**Abstract.** Agents could not find salt deposits, and parameter sweeps did not help. The report shows why from 2D search theory and surveys how animals actually locate licks (20:5-31).
**Key findings.** The **2D narrow-capture law**: encounter rate scales with target size only as 1/ln(d/a). It predicts 1.33× for 5× area, against a measured 1.21-1.56× (20:41-60; Bénichou & Voituriez 2014). Real animals locate licks by **memory and tradition, not gradients**: Poole et al. 2010 goats travel 17.3 km and bypass closer licks; Polansky et al. 2015 elephants; Nevitt 2008 (20:103-142). Bell & Sly 1983: detection threshold depends on need (20:144-157). SDD halo λ=√(D/k) (Wartlick et al. 2009) (20:256-287). NetLogo Ants lower detection threshold 0.05 (20:299-306). Bénichou et al. 2011 intermittent search (20:320-328). Cardé & Willis 2008: real plumes give no direction (20:332-339). Lévy-flight optimality is contested (Viswanathan 1999 vs Palyulin 2014) (20:347-350).
**Decision.** A small halo (R≈4-8), stock-scaled emission, and a binary read (20:403-466). Place memory and social `salt_here` are named as the literature-supported complements (20:493-529). Traps include "a cue that outlives its stock" and "tuning λ until the gate binds" (20:533-569).

### 21 — Mating effort as a drive
**Abstract.** Asks whether libido should be an accumulator that fills and discharges. The answer is no. It should be a baseline drive with a decaying post-mating inhibition and a graded energy ramp, scaled by one gene (21:5-31, 21:369-421).
**Key findings.** Modern sexual-motivation theory treats it as an incentive-gated central motive state, not a reservoir (Ågmo 1999; Toates 2009; Ventura-Aquino & Ågmo 2023) (21:188-200). The **Coolidge effect** rules out a tank: a novel partner reinstates mating in a satiated male (Wilson et al. 1963; Fiorino et al. 1997) (21:212-228). Hormones are "permissive, not triggers" (Schneider 2004) (21:202-210). The energy gate reads fuel *flux* and hits appetitive behaviour first (Schneider & Wade 1989; Klingerman 2011) (21:230-249). Mating effort is heritable, h² roughly 0.2-0.35 (Manning 1961; Snowder 2002; Quirino 2004) (21:283-303). Kokko & Jennions 2008: let each sex's time-out *evolve* (21:174-184).
**Decision.** The probe found the gate shut on 52.6% of adult ticks (male 29.9%, female 82.6%), entirely by construction (21:81-134). Paternity was undercounted for fathers who died during gestation (21:127-134). The incentive (mate-present) term is "the best-sourced change" but was deferred (21:422-432). "No ALife precedent with a measured evolutionary result for an evolvable mating drive" (21:517-519).

### 22 — Sodium appetite dynamics
**Abstract.** Covers when the drive should switch on, how a remembered empty source should be forgotten, and which number measures scarcity (22:5-50).
**Key findings.** Onset lags deficit in naive animals (Michell 1989; Stricker et al. 1991) (22:66-76). **Sensitisation**: after "three or four successive depletions" appetite rises "seemingly lifelong" (Hurley et al. 2014; Morris et al. 2008) (22:101-111). Sodium's reproductive cost sits in lactation, not conception (Suttle 2010; Seynaeve et al. 1996 2.03× oestrus delay) (22:114-212). **Forgetting**: Ranc et al. 2021 blocked roe-deer feeding sites. They fitted two decay rates: value t½ 0.9 d and place t½ 5.6 d, and "never totally forgotten" (22:221-234). Rea et al. 2021: moose kept visiting decommissioned licks for years (22:236-245). Thomson 1996: the update is asymmetric (22:247-257). EWMA β≈0.48-0.60 (Van Allsburg & Shahan 2024) (22:259-266). Bouton 2004: extinction is not erasure (22:276-282). PREE persistence scales as ≈1/p (22:284-292). Lima 1986 and McNamara & Houston 1990: size a buffer by expected interruption (22:442-450).
**Decision.** Widen the need-onset spacing, sized by first-passage time (22:416-481). Memory rule: down-weight toward a floor, asymmetric, no recovery term, count omissions (22:483-513). Instrument: the fraction of the population past a deficit threshold, with r=−0.86 in moose (22:394-403, 22:533-537).

### 23 — Contests, territory and injury
**Abstract.** A source audit of every constant in the contest/territory/give packages, under the rule "a number with no source and no (proposed) marker is a hard stop" (23:12). 83 DOIs verified (23:3).
**Key findings.** Parker 1974 defines RHP as fighting ability and puts ownership *outside* it. The design counted residency twice (23:134-206). Vieira & Peixoto 2013 null: no trait class outweighs another, so equal weights is the honest default (23:226-246). Kokko et al. 2006: over 1,000 parameter draws, 59.2% commonsense, 39.8% no-respect, 1.0% paradoxical; V and C cannot be constants (23:274-306). Kemp & Wiklund 2004: residency is not arbitrary (23:335-349). Stuart-Fox 2006: asymmetry explains r²=0.06-0.13 of escalation, and loser ability beats asymmetry (23:407-430). Hawk-Dove trap: the escalating fraction is (V/C)², not V/C (23:444-451). Enquist & Leimar 1990: what controls lethal escalation is V_resource/V_future (23:457-474). Yan et al. 2024: after a loss P(win) ≈ 0.33 (23:657-668). Koolhaas et al. 2017: the loser's stress decays about 2× slower, with the same peak (23:695-713). Potts et al. 2013 fox active scent time 5.07/3.37 d, and the published models do **not** diffuse the mark (23:766-803).
**Decision.** The "SOURCED" claim-decay band was traced to a designer's tick arithmetic laundered through one citation hop (23:749-763). The ruled diffusion destroys territoriality by about 100× (23:819-853). Replace the `θ_surplus` gate with a marginal-value comparison that deletes the constant (23:981-1001). The full constants table is at 23:1075-1111 and the list of "what would be invented" is at 23:1171-1211.

### 24 — Loser effects, denominators, give rates, severed-inheritance null
**Abstract.** A follow-up to 23 on four rulings (24:18-101).

## 2. Cross-report findings that transfer to the EBM lab

Format: **Claim** — source — *Inference (mapping)*.

1. **Self-model built from received outcomes, not self-reported traits.** — 16:45-54, 16:117-137. *Inference:* a module's "confidence" in a connection should be an EMA of how often the connections it triggered were **answered** downstream, never a stored property. 16:281-284 is the warning: if the outcome term goes to zero, the self-model collapses into a smoothed copy of a raw signal.

2. **Genesis already specified the EBM successor of a hand-built variable.** — 16:367-382: fold interoceptive channels into the same predicted-future vector the energy model minimizes surprise over; predict social responses *before* acting so the update is a prediction error; read out per-channel precision. The retirement test is EBM-on vs the hand-coded fallback. *Inference:* a direct template for the lab. Each small EBM's "action cache" entry should store the predicted outcome so the sleep retraining can fit to error, not to raw reward.

3. **Capacity is inherited, content is learned and reset.** — 16:108-113; 15:65 (template resets at birth, "nothing about what is learned is inherited"). *Inference:* across generations, pass down module architectures and learning-rate hyperparameters. Do not pass down the cached connection contents unless you deliberately test a Lamarckian arm.

4. **Ontogeny as a gated growth curve with a two-sided cost.** — 14:104-117 (von Bertalanffy rate × nutrition EMA), 14:290-297 (one-sided cost pushes the gene to its floor). *Inference:* if the network grows, tie the growth rate to a slow EMA of success and charge a real cost for growing. Also give a real cost for *staying small*, or selection will just minimize growth.

5. **Do not make the master clock evolvable.** — 14:223-228. *Inference:* keep the wake/sleep cycle length and generation length as fixed physics. Let one evolvable time constant scale everything and it becomes "the free-energy trap applied to time itself".

6. **Flat encodings cannot grow structure; generative ones can.** — 19:26-32 (Sims, Framsticks, CPPN-NEAT exist "because their authors hit exactly this wall"). *Inference:* a growing connection graph needs a generative rule, such as "each connection makes another connection". A fixed-length parameter vector will not get there. This directly supports the user's recursive-connection idea.

7. **Every new body/physics dial will be exploited.** — 19:11, 19:20, 19:45-53. *Inference:* if module energy (in the EBM sense) or a "cost per connection" is computed in code, expect connection patterns that farm it. Add conservation tests (19:108-118) before evolving anything: bounded fields, a mass lock, no unclipped refunds.

8. **Memory consolidation and forgetting: two timescales, asymmetric, never delete.** — 22:221-234 (Ranc: value t½ 0.9 d vs place t½ 5.6 d), 22:247-257 (Thomson: one good encounter adds, a run of bad ones deletes), 22:259-266 (EWMA β≈0.5), 22:276-282 (Bouton: extinction is new learning, not erasure). *Inference:* this is the best plasticity/consolidation content in my slice. Cached connections should carry a fast "is it paying" weight and a slow "does it exist" weight. Sleep retraining should down-weight failed connections toward a floor, not prune them to zero, and should count failures, not elapsed time (PREE, 22:284-292).

9. **Sensitisation: repeated stress shifts a set-point permanently.** — 22:101-111 ("three or four successive depletions ... seemingly lifelong"). *Inference:* a candidate rule for sleep retraining. A module that hits the same deficit several times gets a permanently earlier trigger. This gives a cohort heterogeneity that a single global threshold cannot.

10. **Drives are bounded inhibitions plus incentive gating, not tanks.** — 21:188-228 (Coolidge effect falsifies the reservoir), 21:202-210 ("permissive, not triggers"). *Inference:* if an action-drive accumulates during wakefulness and discharges on action, it will produce unbounded urgency. A decaying post-action inhibition that returns to baseline is the sourced shape, gated by whether a suitable stimulus is present.

11. **Perception through a medium with Weber noise, and the resampling leak.** — 13:9, 13:41-47, 13:103. *Inference:* if modules read each other's states directly, you have "telepathy" and selection will be inflated (13:96). Put noise on the read side, proportional to magnitude. Beware the CLT leak: repeated reads of the same source average the noise away unless a "first impression" is cached.

12. **Reliability-weighted vs flat-weighted integration.** — 18:13 (Ernst & Banks 2002), 18:35. *Inference:* when a connection pattern fuses several small EBMs' outputs, weight each by its recent precision. Fixed weights are an approximation, not a default.

13. **Contests between modules: what actually decides them.** — 23:226-246 (no trait class dominates; equal weights), 23:407-430 (loser's own state predicts escalation better than asymmetry, so self-assessment beats mutual assessment), 24:109-127 (losing lowers *willingness to engage*, not ability), 24:169-175 (loser-effect duration evolves independently of ability), 23:695-713 (losing shows up as slower recovery, not a bigger spike). *Inference:* if modules compete for the right to fire or claim a connection, a losing module should get a decaying reluctance to re-compete, set by its own state. Do not cut its competence. Mesterton-Gibbons (24:184-192): never ship a winner effect without the matching loser effect.

14. **Claim/territory fields: diffusion destroys ownership.** — 23:819-853, 23:790-796 (published territorial models expire marks, they do not diffuse them). *Inference:* if connections claim regions of a shared space, use deposit-plus-expiry. Diffusing claims makes everything overlap ("each halo overlaps ~28 others").

15. **Detection halos, intermittent search, memory beats gradients.** — 20:41-60 (log law: making the target bigger barely helps), 20:320-328 (a halo changes the search class), 20:103-142. *Inference:* if new connections must "find" partners in a sparse space, enlarging receptive fields buys only logarithmic gains. Caching the locations of past successful connections (the user's "cache") is the literature-backed lever.

16. **Open-endedness and diversity measurement.** — 19:71-85 (archive bins grow exponentially with axes; coverage crashes as an artifact; replace axes rather than add them; CVT-MAP-Elites), 17:35-39 (free recombination gives cycling, not stable conventions), 19:12 (Polyworld plateaued). *Inference:* measure diversity of connection patterns with a fixed centroid budget, or the metric will lie as descriptors are added.

## 3. Methodology lessons

- **Name the experimental unit; denominators lie.** One individual produced 138 of 152 escalations (90.8%). The per-contest rate was a pseudoreplication artifact. Report both rates and score the per-individual one (24:373-403). Log the events that did *not* happen (turn-aways), or the denominator is the 5% Turner kept (23:323-333, 23:1164-1167). *For the EBM lab:* one hyperactive module can dominate "connection rate"; report per-module rates and a concentration statistic `max_i/Σ` (24:401-403).
- **Severed-inheritance null (pref-redraw).** Redraw the offspring's gene from the population distribution so phenotypic variance is kept and additive transmission is killed. Only this tests "inherited"; shuffle and off arms cannot (24:546-581). The cheap substitute is parent-offspring regression (24:600-602). Pre-register it as a manipulation check, since `r_PT ∝ h_P` makes the result analytic (24:603-606).
- **Cross-fostering breaks perfect collinearity.** "Learned from parent" and "inherited from parent" are the same individual, so randomize the learning source (15:124-130). *EBM lab:* if child networks are warm-started from parent caches, randomize which parent's cache they get to separate the genetic effect from the cached one.
- **Redraw-at-birth plus placebo loci plus patch-conditioning** to separate behaviour from kinship and ecology (17:193-207). Placebo-locus baselines are a cheap universal control.
- **Dilution vs displacement.** Adding any input to a weighted sum lowers each input's correlation with the outcome mechanically. Track the weight gene's own trajectory (18:141).
- **Uniform-multiplier no-op.** A modulator applied equally to every row of an argmax changes nothing (16:210-213, 21:491-495, 13:121). Two genes on one row are uninterpretable alone (Hill-Robertson) (21:491-495).
- **Selectability floor.** At N≈40 a gene needs a ≳1.25% fitness effect to beat drift. Expect nulls and pre-register them as likely (13:65, 17:156-157, 21:120-122). Boundary pileup is not selection, so check it against the null (18:130-133, 21:528-529).
- **Laundered constants.** Trace provenance. A tick-arithmetic guess became "SOURCED" via one citation hop (23:749-763). Mark every unsourced magnitude "(proposed)" (21:355-363, 22:478-481).
- **Static algebra vs dynamic reality.** A provable exploit was only break-even under day/night cycling. Test invariants dynamically (19:47, 19:114).
- **Threshold, not gradient.** A flat response may mean every condition tested sits above a threshold. Sweep below the minimum before concluding "no coupling" (22:405-410, Cury et al. 2011).
- **Corrections and retractions recorded in-text:** Riolo et al. year (17:26). Mousseau & Roff mean (14:68-71). Sánchez-Tójar's publication bias against the bib textbook result (17:49-51). Rutte et al.'s "5×/2×" not found in that paper (23:664-666). Kokko et al. DOI confusion (23:276). Machlis DOI resolving to Wickler (24:351-353). An AI fetch summariser "produced plausible-sounding" wording to be "treat[ed] as fabricated" (24:705). Report 24 overturned its own earlier session's `DELTA` ruling (24:44). Report 22 falsified the premise of a shipped no-decay memory rule (22:506-508, 22:623-639).
